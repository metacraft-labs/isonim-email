# Email visual review brief (static part)

Static half of the review brief. The generated
"expected on the screenshot" block is appended below this
file's content for each (story, family, viewport, scheme); this part
carries the product voice, design goals, rubric and report format.

## 1. What you are reviewing

An HTML email rendered in one mail client. These are transactional
emails — receipts, invoices, alerts and notifications the recipient
did not ask to admire but needs to trust: the voice is plain,
specific and calm, numbers and dates are exact, and every message
does one job (confirm, warn, ask) with a single obvious next step.
Judge the screenshot as that recipient, in that client's chrome,
not as a design comp.

## 2. Design goals

Hierarchy first: the one thing the message is about dominates, the
supporting detail recedes, the footer stays quiet. Spacing follows a
steady vertical rhythm with generous separation between sections.
Brand colours mark only the accents (logo, primary button, key
figures); body copy stays near-black on a light ground. Every email
must stay legible and tappable on a 375 px phone, with columns
stacked and no horizontal scrolling. Dark mode is accommodated, not
designed: nothing may become illegible or garish where the client
applies it, but no separate dark artwork is expected.

## 4. What to evaluate

Alignment, spacing, typography, colour, visual weight and polish;
mobile stacking (columns stack, tables degrade to their declared
mobile form, nothing overflows); dark-mode legibility where the
family honours dark; images-off legibility (alt text carries the
meaning where images are blocked).

## 5. Report format

Under 200 words. Start with "Expected elements: present |
missing-X | replaced-by-Y". Anything missing means a rating of 4 or
below. Then the issues with their locations, then the 1–2 top
fixes, then a rating out of 10. Severity per issue: P1 =
information lost or wrong, P2 = broken layout or illegible, P3 =
visibly off-design, P4 = polish.
